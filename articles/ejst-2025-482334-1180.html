Moving Towards a Zero-Waste Future: Assessment of Behaviour, Knowledge, and Perception of Community of Delhi Towards Organic Household Waste Management

Mariam Amir

Archive home / Issue 2
Volume: 5 (2025), Issue 2Pages: 219-234DOI: 10.48309/ejst.2025.482334.1180

Abstract

Waste management is becoming increasingly crucial as the world population continues to rise and urbanize, resulting in greater consumption and waste generation. The concept of "zero-waste" has emerged as a possible option in this context. Using a holistic and sustainable approach to resource management, zero- waste strives to reduce the quantity of garbage transferred to landfills or incinerators. To achieve zero-waste, however, considerable changes in consumer behavior, industrial practices, and waste management infrastructure are required. The purpose of this study was to analyze the level of environmental concern of the public towards waste disposal and their willingness to contribute and take approaches towards the zero-waste concept starting from their own households. A set of questionnaires were sent to 141 targeted respondents, through social media platforms such as WhatsApp, and in-person interviews were conducted. The respondent target focused on the community from every educational background and age group staying in both houses and apartments. Inferential analysis with chi-square tests revealed a significant association between employment status and awareness of zero-waste techniques, implying that working professionals are more likely to be knowledgeable about these practices. The gender-based study found that female respondents were more likely to follow prospective zero-waste behavior than male respondents. The study suggests that insufficient waste management infrastructure, a lack of awareness, and limited community engagement impede successful organic waste management. Implementing educational programs, social media campaigns, and improving waste management infrastructure are among the recommendations made to promote zero-waste behavior and increase public participation.
