In Vivo and In Silico Evaluation of Analgesic and Hypoglycemic Activities of Methanolic Extract of Dalbergia stipulacea Roxb. on Male Swiss-Albino Mice

Nilufar Sultana; Anika Shikder; Md. Rakibul Islam; Ishtiaq Ahmed Tahim

Volume 5 (2025), Issue 2 · Pages 195-218

DOI: 10.48309/ejst.2025.482687.1181

Download PDF

Abstract

Dalbergia stipulacea is a medicinal plant with a long history of traditional use. Recent scientific investigations have focused on its potential as a source of novel analgesic and hypoglycemic agents. To evaluate the plant’s therapeutic potential, in vivo and in silico studies were conducted. Methanolic extracts of D. stipulacea were administered to mice to assess their analgesic and hypoglycemic effects. A study utilizing in-silico molecular docking and ADME was carried out to evaluate the oral bioavailability and binding affinity of the compounds from Dalbergia stipulacea. Methanolic extracts (ME) were used in the study at doses of 200 mg/kg and 400 mg/kg to evaluate the analgesic and hypoglycemic effects. In the acetic acid-induced writhing test, ME (200 mg/kg) and ME (400 mg/kg) demonstrated significant results, scoring 46.4% and 56.8% of inhibition respectively. In the tail immersion test, ME at a 400 mg/kg dosage resulted in the largest delay time increase of 14.76 ± 0.68 at 120 minutes. A substantial reduction in plasma glucose levels was noted in the streptozotocin-induced hypoglycemia test for 200 mg/kg and 400 mg/kg of methanolic extract. In-silico studies utilizing molecular docking have further elucidated the mechanisms underlying these effects. The in-silico investigation revealed that Dalpulapan D & R, R- velucarpin A had the best docking score of around -10.8 kcal/mol against Cox2, as compared with diclofenac's -7.2 kcal/mol. However, as compared to Glibenclamide (- 9.2 kcal/mol), R-velucarpin C demonstrated the highest docking score (-10.9 kcal/mol) against the sulfonylurea receptor.
