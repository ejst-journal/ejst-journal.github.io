Corrosion Inhibition Studies of Dioscorea Bulbifera Leaves Extract on Aluminum Metal in 0.6 M Hydrochloric Acid Solution

Ayuba Abdullahi Muhammad; Sagir Isa Musa

Archive home / Issue 4

Pages: 299-308   DOI: 10.48309/ejst.2026.579001.1313

Abstract

This study evaluates the corrosion inhibition performance of Dioscorea bulbifera leaf extract on aluminum in 0.6 M HCl using weight loss (ΔW) measurements at 303–323 K over 4 hours. The results show that increasing extract concentration (0.0–0.6 g) significantly reduces ΔW and corrosion rate (CR) at all temperatures. At 303 K, CR decreased from 0.035 to 0.029, while inhibition efficiency (%IE) increased slightly from 62% to approximately 63– 64%, indicating effective surface protection. A similar trend was observed at 313 K and 323 K, where CR decreased with concentration, accompanied by slight improvements in %IE. Surface coverage (θ) also increased marginally, confirming enhanced adsorption of inhibitor molecules on the aluminum surface. However, temperature had a pronounced effect on inhibition performance. As temperature increased from 303 to 323 K, CR increased while %IE decreased from 62 to 43% at the blank concentration, indicating reduced inhibitor efficiency. This decline is attributed to thermal desorption of adsorbed molecules at higher temperatures. The behavior suggests that the inhibition mechanism is predominantly governed by physisorption, involving weak intermolecular interactions. Thus, Dioscorea bulbifera extract exhibits moderate %IE, with optimal performance at lower temperatures
