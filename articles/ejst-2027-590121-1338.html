Comparative Thermal and Aerodynamic Performance Assessment of Chimney Systems with Different Materials and Cross-Sectional Geometries Using Simulation

Figen Balo; Ahmet Karahan

Archive home / Issue 1
Volume: 7 (2027), Issue 1Pages: 1-22DOI: 10.48309/ejst.2027.590121.1338

Abstract

The purpose of this study is to provide a comparative perspective on how building materials and cross-sectional geometry affect the thermal properties and aerodynamic performance of chimney systems. Alternative designs (eight in total: AISI 316L stainless steel, cast steel, welded steel, coated with refractory aluminum, and flexible steel pipe) were tested in circular and oval arrangements. KesaAladin was used for simulations based on the European EN 13384 calculation framework under a set of identical boiler operating and climate conditions for a natural gas heating system in Samsun, Türkiye. Pressure, flow velocity, temperature, gas density, and acoustic characteristics were investigated. The stainless-steel configuration had the highest chimney flow velocity (2.78 m/s) and flue-gas velocity (3.71 m/s) along with the maximum negative-pressure magnitude (66.4 Pa). Flex-steel reached the lowest corresponding velocities (i.e., 2.29 and 3.14 m/s, respectively) and the lowest maximum negative-pressure magnitude (60.7 Pa). The range of flue-gas temperatures was 156-158 °C, while the inner-wall temperature ranged from 118 to 131 °C. Generally speaking, the changes between circular and oval configurations were modest in comparison with the changes from the materials. These results illustrate why the choice of chimney configuration must account for material-dependent thermal and aerodynamic characteristics. The paper presents a universal, comparative engineering appraisal of alternative chimney designs without demonstrating energy savings or emissions reductions.

Keywords

Chimney system; Energy performance; Energy saving in buildings; Environmental pollution; KesaAladin software
