Computational Insights into the Corrosion Inhibition Potential of 9-Octadecenal on Mild Steel: A DFT and Molecular Dynamics Study

Muhammad Shuaibu; Abdullahi Muhammad Ayuba; Bishir Usman

Volume 5 (2025), Issue 4 · Pages 322-342

DOI: 10.48309/ejst.2025.502833.1212

Download PDF

Abstract

The inhibitory effect of 9-Octadecenal (9AL) on the corrosion of mild steel (Fe(110)) was assessed using quantum chemical computations and molecular dynamics (MD) simulations. Various quantum chemical parameters, including EHOMO, ELUMO, energy gap (ΔE), dipole moment (μ), global hardness (η), global softness (σ), absolute electronegativity (χ), and the fraction of electrons transferred (ΔN) were computed. The results illustrated the molecule’s ability to both donate and accept electrons, with Fukui function analysis identifying the terminal carbon (C1) as the key nucleophilic site and the conjugated double bond (C9) as the main electrophilic site. In addition, the second Fukui function provided quantitative insights, showing nucleophilic and electrophilic contributions of 39.62% and 58.49%, respectively. MD simulations indicated strong adsorption, characterized by an average binding energy of 191.03±14.63 kcal/mol, consistent with chemisorption. The flat adsorption geometry of the molecule on the Fe(110) surface promoted optimal surface coverage and the formation of a compact, stable protective layer. These findings suggest that 9-Octadecenal is a viable and eco-friendly corrosion inhibitor, with further experimental research needed to confirm its potential for industrial use.
