Comparison of the Accuracy of Three Instruments (APACHEII, SOFA, and MPMII) in Predicting the Mortality of Patients in Intensive Care Units

Naser Mozaffari; Gholamreza Hamidkholgh; Mahzad Yousefian; Shabnam Salehi

Archive home / Issue 1

Pages: 96-106   DOI: 10.48309/ejst.2026.547093.1265

Abstract

Accurate prediction of mortality in intensive care units (ICUs) is vital for patient management and resource allocation. Among the most widely used scoring systems are APACHE II, SOFA, and MPM II, each providing valuable but variable estimates of patient outcomes. The present study aimed to compare the accuracy of these three instruments in predicting mortality in ICU patients. This cross-sectional study (2020) included 150 ICU patients from Ardabil University hospitals. Eligible participants were adults (>18 years) with ICU stays ≥24 hours, excluding burn and cardiac surgery cases. Data were gathered using demographic forms and APACHE II, SOFA, and MPM II questionnaires. Statistical analyses (SPSS v23) applied descriptive statistics, t-test, correlation, sensitivity, and specificity. Among the studied patients, 85 were male, with a mean age of 60.74 ± 19.62 years. The average duration of hospitalization was 10.17 ± 10.34 days, and 52 patients died. Logistic regression showed that hospital type, gender, age, length of hospitalization, duration of intubation, diagnosis, and GCS were not significant predictors of mortality. Predicted mortality was 32.21% with APACHE II, 32.50% with SOFA, and 64.01% with MPM II. Sensitivity and specificity values were 83% and 48% for APACHE II, 79% and 46% for SOFA, and 73% and 50% for MPM II, respectively. In conclusion, APACHE II demonstrated higher accuracy compared with SOFA and MPM II, and its application is recommended as the preferred prognostic tool in ICU settings
