Spin-Encoded Reactivity in Monovalent Boron: Substituent-Imprinted Singlet-Triplet Signatures of Borylenes

Mehdi Nabati

Archive home / Issue 4

Pages: 309-330   DOI: 10.48309/ejst.2026.578182.1308

Abstract

A systematic quantum chemical investigation was carried out on sixteen monovalent B–X derivatives to elucidate substituent-controlled stabilization, electronic structure, and magnetic response in both singlet and triplet states. Calculated singlet–triplet energy gaps (ΔES-T) reveal that several substituents strongly favor singlet stabilization, with larger positive ΔE values directly correlating with enhanced thermodynamic stability. Analysis of σB-X bond energies demonstrates that stronger bonding interactions generally accompany increased singlet preference, establishing a clear energetic- structural relationship. Natural bond orbital (NBO) analysis shows that the boron lone pair (LP) in stabilized singlets possesses significant s-character, indicating pronounced electron localization rather than pure p-type behavior. Spin density calculations confirm efficient spin delocalization in triplet states, while singlets exhibit reduced magnetic perturbation at the boron center. Magnetic shielding calculations further reveal systematic variations in 11B- NMR chemical shifts consistent with changes in electronic density and bonding strength. Additionally, computed dipole moments and isotropic/anisotropic polarizabilities demonstrate that substituents modulate not only stability, but also electronic deformability and charge distribution. Overall, this study establishes quantitative correlations between ΔES-T, σB-X bond strength, hybridization patterns, NMR response, dipole moments, and polarizability, providing a unified electronic framework for understanding substituent-driven stabilization in B-centered systems

Keywords

Singlet-triplet energy gap, Boron lone pair hybridization, σB-X bond energy, 11B-NMR spectroscopy, Polarizability
