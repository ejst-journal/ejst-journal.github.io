Harnessing the Power of Combination of Herbal Extracts against UTI Pathogens

Amruta Prakashrao Kanakdande; Shubham Pathade

Archive home / Issue 3

Pages: 287-293   DOI: 10.48309/ejst.2026.570634.1301

Abstract

Antibacterial efficacy through a synergistic approach of selective herbal extracts, i.e., amla (Emblica officinalis) druhalad (Berberis aristata) and brahmi (Bacopa monnieri) is evaluated to discover an alternative approach to control urinary-tract infections (UTIs) caused by common uropathogenic bacteria. These herbs are extracted using a Soxhlet extraction method in presence of methanol and water as solvents. The separate extracts of all medicinal herbs in both the solvents are evaluated for their antibacterial potential against common uropathogens, isolated from UTI patients. Based on morphological and biochemical tests, two microorganisms were identified as i.e., Bacillus subtilis and Staphylococcus aureus. On the basis of 16S rRNA sequencing techniques, the one was identified as Kosakonia cowanii (OQ 073698), alongwith the yeast Candida albicans. Overall, significant zones of inhibition are recorded against all the uropathogens considered under study upon amla:daruhalad 7:3 (w/w) ratio in water extract with extracted yield 9.0 g/250 mL. One of the core findings from the present investigation includes the uropathogen Kosakonia cowanii is resistant to ampicillin while the above- selected screened combination of extracts is an effective and alternative treatment approach for ampicillin resistant uropathogens. This synergistic effect achieved with the tested extract combinations can provide a starting point for the development of alternative formulations, effective against the antibiotic resistant Kosakonia cowanii (OQ073698) isolate which would be effective against other uropathogens. Additionally, it was proven that these edible plant extracts could be used as natural alternative inhibitors to control microbial pathogenesis related to UTIs
