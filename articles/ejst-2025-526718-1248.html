Antioxidant and Anti-Anaemic Potential of Methanolic and Aqueous Extracts of Root Samples: A Comparative Analysis of Beetroot, Carrot, Sweet Potato, and Radish

Sadhnakumari Chourasiya; Saloni Desai

Archive home / Issue 4
Volume: 5 (2025), Issue 4Pages: 424-440DOI: 10.48309/ejst.2025.526718.1248

Abstract

Anaemia is one of the disorders that can develop as a result of oxidative stress, which is brought on by an excess of free radicals. Antioxidants are essential for reducing oxidative damage and neutralising free radicals. This study sought to determine the flavonoid and carotenoid content as well as the antioxidant activity of methanolic and aqueous extracts from the roots. The DPPH (2,2-diphenyl-1-picrylhydrazyl) radical scavenging assay, was employed to examine the antioxidant activity. The calibration curve regression equation was used to compute the total flavonoid content, which was measured spectrophotometrically using a quercetin standard. The results showed high linearity, with y = 0.007x + 0.029 and a correlation coefficient of r = 0.996. To determine whether bioactive molecules with antioxidant relevance were present, the amount of carotenoid content was also assessed. Statistical analysis was used to compare the phytochemical contents and antioxidant capabilities of the extracts. The study revealed that sweet potato extracts contained the highest flavonoid content. However, all root samples showed appreciable levels of carotenoids and demonstrated the significant antioxidant activity as evidenced by DPPH assay results. These findings suggest that both extracts, irrespective of solvent type, possess notable antioxidant properties. The presence of flavonoids and carotenoids—compounds known for their roles in supporting red blood cell formation and protecting against oxidative damage—indicates that these root vegetables may serve as effective natural agents for preventing or managing anaemia. The flavonoid and carotenoid contents were noteworthy, highlighting that these roots are rich sources of bioactive metabolites. These compounds may contribute not only to antioxidant activity, but also to potential anti-anaemic effects, given their roles in enhancing overall physiological functions. Therefore, it can be concluded that the selected roots are promising natural sources of antioxidants and may also exhibit anti-anaemic properties.

Keywords

Anaemia, Antioxidants assay, Flavonoids, Phytochemical tests, Thin layer chromatography, Carotenoids.
