Surgical Management of Posterolateral Tibial Plateau Fractures: A Comparative Study of Direct Lateral and Posterolateral Approaches

Reza Noktehsanj; Ali Nami; Aziz Kamran; Amin Najjar Khodabakhsh

Archive home / Issue 1

Pages: 74-83   DOI: 10.48309/ejst.2026.545126.1264

Abstract

Posterolateral tibial plateau fractures are challenging injuries of the knee joint, which is essential for weight-bearing and stability. These fractures typically occur due to high- energy trauma and involve complex surgical management due to the proximity of neurovascular structures and surrounding ligaments. This study aims to compare the clinicaloutcomes of two surgicalapproaches—direct lateral and posterolateral—for the fixation of posterolateral tibial plateau fractures. A descriptive was conducted on 54 patients who were evenly divided into two groups. One group underwent the direct lateral approach, while the other received the posterolateral approach. The primary outcomes measured included the International Knee Documentation Committee (IKDC) score, Lysholm score, range of motion, neurovascular injury, fixation success, and quality of joint reduction. Patients treated with the direct lateral approach showed significantly higher IKDC scores (92 ± 2.4) compared to the posterolateral group (90 ± 1.3), with p < 0.001. Similarly, the Lysholm scores were better in the lateral group (96 ± 2.4) versus the posterolateral group (88 ± 1.5), p < 0.001. The range of knee motion was also superior in the direct lateral group (130° vs. 125°, p < 0.001). Neurovascular injuries were fewer in the direct lateral approach. The modified direct lateral approach offers better clinical outcomes compared to the posterolateral approach, minimizing neurovascular risks and improving joint reduction and fixation. This method provides a safer and more effective surgical option for managing posterolateral tibial plateau fractures
