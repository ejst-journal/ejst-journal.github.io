Beyond Adhesives: The Multidimensional Role of Colophony in Industry and Medicine

Geetika Shrivastav; Anubhav Dubey; Mamta Kumari

Archive home / Issue 2

Pages: 120-134   DOI: 10.48309/ejst.2026.550990.1271

Abstract

Colophony, commonly known as rosin, is a natural resin derived primarily from coniferous trees like pines. It is composed predominantly of resin acids, particularly abietic acid, along with a neutral fraction. This complex mixture serves as a versatile industrial raw material, with applications in adhesives, cosmetics, paints, inks, soldering fluxes, and medical products. Its eco-friendly properties, renewability, and affordability have earned it the designation green oil. Despite its extensive usage, colophony poses potential health risks, including allergic contact dermatitis (ACD) and occupational asthma, primarily due to its oxidized resin acids. Diagnosis and prevention of these adverse effects remain challenging. Modified forms of colophony, while reducing some allergenic components, may introduce new sensitizers like maleopimaric acid. Colophony also demonstrates significant pharmacological activities. Its derivatives exhibit wound healing, cytotoxicity, antimicrobial, anti-mosquito, and spasmolytic properties. Research highlights the antimicrobial potential of resin acid derivatives against bacteria such as S. aureus and E. faecalis, as well as its anti-mosquito efficacy through insect growth-regulating compounds. The chemical diversity of colophony is influenced by its source and extraction method, with α-pinene being the primary monoterpene in natural varieties. However, synthetic and regional differences contribute to varied chemical profiles. Future research should address the prevalence of ACD, optimize allergen detection, and explore additional therapeutic applications.
