Comparing the Results of Culture and Antibiogram of Tracheal Secretions of Intubated Patients One Year before COVID-19 with the Last One Year in Patients Admitted Somaieh Matin1

Vahid Adiban; Fatemeh Mirershadi; Reza Sarem

Volume 5 (2025), Issue 4 · Pages 441-449

DOI: 10.48309/ejst.2025.533141.1255

Download PDF

Abstract

Background: Ventilator-associated infections are among the most important infections in hospitalized patients in intensive care units (ICUs). Therefore, it is crucial to investigate the potential changes in microbial patterns and antibiotic resistance in hospitalized patients in ICUs during the COVID-19 pandemic which is the aim of this study. Method: In this cross-sectional study, 126 patients who had cultures requested from their endotracheal tube secretions were included, covering one year before the onset of COVID-19 and the most recent year. All collected data from checklists were analyzed using statistical methods in SPSS Version 24. Results: Cultures obtained from endotracheal tube secretions were positive in 70% of patients. The most commonly identified microbial species were Acinetobacter (50%) and Klebsiella (20%). Conclusion: The observed differences in resistance profiles highlight the need for specific interventions and targeted surveillance to effectively manage antibiotic resistance.
